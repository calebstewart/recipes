Harissa Baked Feta Pasta

An adaptation of Delish's baked feta pasta, reworked for meal prep, with harissa in place of the red pepper flakes and chickpeas and sausage to make it a full dinner. The core idea driving every change: make the sauce ahead, cook pasta fresh each night. Pasta does not survive storage in sauce; everything else does.

Instructions

  1. Rack in center, oven to 400°. Combine tomatoes, shallot, garlic, harissa, thyme, and all but 1 Tbsp. oil in a large ovenproof skillet or baking dish. Salt, toss.
  2. Nest feta in center; drizzle with reserved 1 Tbsp. oil. Bake until tomatoes burst and feta is golden, 40–45 min.
  3. Meanwhile, brown sausage in a skillet over medium, breaking into bite-size pieces, 8–10 min. Set aside. Drain, rinse, and pat chickpeas dry.
  4. Mash feta, tomatoes, shallot, and garlic with a fork until the sauce comes together.
  5. Fold in chickpeas. Simmer gently over low heat, stirring, ~10 min, until creamy and sauce-soaked. (Or cover loosely with foil, back into the 400° oven 10–15 min.) Splash in water if it tightens. Pull it looser than looks right.
  6. While that simmers, boil pasta to al dente. Reserve 1/2 c. pasta water; drain.
  7. Add pasta, sausage, and pasta water to the sauce. Toss until glossy.
  8. Off heat: lemon juice, salt to taste, torn basil.

Notes

  • Use at least 1 1/2 Tbsp. harissa for a prep batch, not less. The flavors meld and deepen over a few days, which suits prep, but capsaicin keeps infusing into the olive oil in the fridge — a batch that's right on day one reads noticeably hotter by day three.
  • Steady 400° the whole way, no high-heat finish. A 450° finishing step belongs to a different version of this recipe and doesn't help here; it scorches the ground spices in the harissa.
  • Dried thyme leaves, in at the start, rather than fresh sprigs. Beyond being what's in the cupboard, it means no woody stems to fish out of a cold sauce before storage — a real annoyance with the sprig version.
  • Lemon juice, not zest. Zest oils fade in the fridge anyway. Acid at the end matters more than usual here: roasted tomatoes plus harissa get rich and heavy over several days, and the lemon snaps it back.
  • Chickpeas get braised into the mashed sauce rather than roasted alongside the tomatoes. Roasting them in means mashing the feta smashes them, and scooping them out with a slotted spoon to mash around them is fussy. Braising gives up some browning but gains much more absorbed flavor, and any crisp edges would be gone by day two in the fridge regardless.
  • Brown the sausage on prep day but store it separately. Cooked meat sitting in a cold sauce for days is the failure mode — it dries out and the fat congeals unpleasantly. Reunited in the pan on eating day, the sausage renders fat that emulsifies into the sauce when tossed with the pasta water. Rotisserie chicken is the obvious shortcut, but it contributes nothing back to the sauce.
  • Store it loose — looser than looks right. Chickpeas keep drinking sauce in the fridge, so a batch that looks perfect on Sunday is stiff by Wednesday.
  • Salt late. Feta, harissa, and sausage all bring salt, and it tastes saltier after sitting.
  • For vegetables: broccolini or green beans into the pasta water for the last 2 min, or baby spinach wilted into the hot sauce. Both on eating day, so they stay bright.
  • If you baked in ceramic, move the sauce to a pot before the step 5 simmer. Ceramic on a burner cracks.

Meatless variant

  • Leave the sausage out entirely and keep the chickpeas. The dish stands up on its own — harissa, feta, and roasted tomatoes were always carrying it.
  • Per serving that lands at about 836 cal, 26 g protein, 43 g fat (13 g saturated), 90 g carbohydrate, 9 g fiber, and 1,161 mg sodium: roughly 275 fewer calories, 9 g less saturated fat, and 630 mg less sodium than the version with sausage.
  • Keep the chickpeas in. They supply about 5 g of the 26 g protein, and without them the dish is mostly pasta and fat.
  • The sausage was doing structural work, not just flavor: its rendered fat emulsified into the sauce. Without it, reserve more pasta water than usual — closer to 3/4 c. — and toss harder off heat to bring the sauce together.
  • A whole 10.8-oz. bag of frozen broccoli florets (Birds Eye Steamfresh), microwaved in the bag, is the side that fits: 105 cal, 7 g protein, 14 g carbohydrate, 7 g fiber, and 70 mg sodium for the whole bag. Dinner comes to about 941 cal with 16 g fiber.
  • Prep is simpler too — no browning step and one less container, since the sausage was the component that had to be stored separately.

Meal prep

  • Make through step 5. Cool. Sauce and chickpeas in one container, sausage in another.
  • Both keep 3–4 days refrigerated.
  • Divide the sauce into 4 portions (~1 cup each) and the sausage into 4 (~3 oz. each).
  • To reheat one serving: boil 3 oz. pasta, reserve ~1/4 c. water. Warm one sauce portion and one sausage portion together in a skillet over low-medium until loose and bubbling. Add the pasta and a splash of water, toss. Finish off heat with lemon and basil.
