Homemade Pasta

Egg pasta dough with semolina for bite and all-purpose flour for silkiness. Firm enough to run through a pasta roller without tearing, and it holds a fettuccine or pappardelle cut cleanly. Makes about 1 1/2 lb. of fresh noodles. Mostly hands-on time: 10 min of kneading, a 30 min rest, then rolling and cutting.

Instructions

  1. Whisk the semolina, all-purpose flour, and salt together on a clean counter or in a wide bowl. Push it into a mound and make a deep, wide well in the center.
  2. Crack the eggs into the well and add the oil. Beat them with a fork, then pull in flour from the inner wall a little at a time until it turns to a shaggy mass. Bring the rest in with your hands or a bench scraper.
  3. Judge the dough: if dry crumbs won't join after a minute of pressing, add water 1 Tbsp. at a time. If it sticks to your hands, dust in a little semolina.
  4. Knead 7–10 min, until smooth, firm, and springs back slowly when poked.
  5. Wrap tightly in plastic and rest at room temperature 30 min.
  6. Cut the dough into quarters. Keep the ones you aren't working with wrapped.
  7. By machine: Flatten one quarter and run it through the widest setting. Fold it in thirds like a letter, turn it a quarter turn, and run it through again. Repeat 3–4 times, until it is a smooth rectangle with clean edges. Then run it through each narrower setting once, until you can see the shadow of your hand through it — thinner for fettuccine or ravioli, a notch thicker for pappardelle.
  8. By hand: Roll each quarter on a lightly floured counter as thin as you can manage, turning and flipping it often so it stays round and doesn't stick.
  9. Lay the sheets on a semolina-dusted towel and let them dry 10–15 min, until leathery but still bendable. This is what keeps the cut strands from welding back together.
  10. Cut: through the cutter attachment, or dust a sheet well, fold it loosely into a flat roll, and slice across with a sharp knife. Shake the strands loose right away.
  11. Toss the noodles in semolina and twirl into loose nests on a sheet pan.
  12. Boil in well-salted water 2–4 min, until tender but with a little chew. Taste from the 2 min mark. Reserve a cup of pasta water before draining, and finish the pasta in its sauce.

Notes

  • Weigh the flour. A cup of semolina can vary by 30 g depending on how it's scooped, and this dough is stiff enough that it doesn't forgive that. For reference, the source's volumes were 1 1/2 c. semolina, 1 1/2 c. all-purpose, and 1/4 c. water.
  • Eggs are the rest of the hydration, and "large" ranges from about 50 to 60 g out of the shell. Aim for about 200 g of beaten egg; if yours come up short, that's when the water in step 3 earns its place.
  • Use fine semolina flour, often labeled semola rimacinata or "pasta flour" (Bob's Red Mill semolina works). Coarse semolina — the cream-of-wheat texture — makes a gritty dough that tears in the roller.
  • Dust with semolina, never all-purpose. All-purpose absorbs into the surface and turns gummy; semolina stays on as grit and keeps strands separate.
  • The dough should feel stiffer than bread dough and will seem too dry at first. Knead a few minutes before deciding it needs water — semolina takes time to hydrate.
  • The rest isn't optional. It relaxes the gluten so the dough rolls out instead of springing back. It can go up to 2 hr at room temperature, or overnight in the fridge (let it warm up 30 min before rolling).
  • Fresh pasta cooks fast and keeps cooking in the sauce, so pull it a hair early.
  • No need to boil sheets for lasagna; layer them in raw.
  • Storage: refrigerate nests in an airtight container up to 1 day — longer and egg pasta turns gray and sticky. To keep it, freeze the nests on the sheet pan until firm, then bag them; good for 3 months. Cook straight from frozen, adding about a minute. Don't thaw first, or they clump.
  • Eight servings is about 3 oz. of fresh pasta each, which is a side or a light plate. For dinner portions with a hearty sauce, call it 6.
  • This egg dough is for ribbons, sheets, and filled pasta. For short extruded shapes, use extruded semolina pasta, which dries for the pantry.
