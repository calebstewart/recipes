# Extruded Semolina Pasta

Semolina and water, with no eggs: the same dough boxed pasta is made from,
pushed through the KitchenAid pasta press as short tubes. It's leaner and
chewier than egg pasta and holds up under a chunky sauce. It also dries hard
and keeps in the pantry, so it works for meal prep in a way fresh egg pasta
can't. Makes about 1 lb. dried, which is six 3-oz. portions.

## Ingredients

- 500 g fine semolina flour (semola rimacinata), plus more for dusting
- 185 g warm water, plus up to 15 g more as needed

## Instructions

1. Put the semolina in the mixer bowl with the flat beater. With the mixer on speed 2, pour in the water in a slow stream.
2. Mix 3–5 min, until it looks like damp pebbles, some pea-sized and some larger. It should not form a smooth ball.
3. Squeeze a handful. It should press into a firm lump that holds together and doesn't feel tacky. If it crumbles back apart, add water 5 g at a time and mix 1 min after each addition.
4. Press the pebbles into a single mass, wrap tightly, and rest 30 min so the semolina finishes absorbing the water.
5. Fit the press with the rigatoni or large macaroni plate. Line two sheet pans with a layer of semolina.
6. Pinch off walnut-sized pieces of dough and squeeze each one firmly into a ball. Keep the rest wrapped as you go, because it dries out quickly.
7. Run the mixer on speed 10. Drop in one ball at a time, adding the next as the last one disappears into the auger.
8. As the pasta comes out, cut it with the wire cutter at about 1 to 1 1/2 in. Let the pieces fall onto the semolina. Re-feed the first ragged inch or two of each run.
9. Toss the pieces in the semolina as they collect so they don't stick together.
10. To cook fresh: boil in well-salted water, tasting from 4 min. Fresh tubes usually take 4–6 min.
11. To store: spread the pasta in a single layer and let it air-dry at room temperature, stirring now and then, until it is hard all the way through and snaps instead of bending. This takes a day or two. Store airtight in the pantry.
12. To cook dried pasta: boil in well-salted water, tasting from 8 min.

## Notes

- The amount of water is a starting point. Tune it after your first batch. The KitchenAid press is fed by the ball, so its dough has to hold a ball shape. That makes it a bit wetter than the crumbly dough automatic extruders use, and much drier than dough for hand-shaped pasta. If strands stick together or smear, use less water next time. If the press strains or the pasta comes out cracked and rough, use more. Write down whatever number works and change it here.
- Use fine semolina flour, often labeled semola rimacinata. Coarse semolina doesn't hydrate evenly and extrudes rough.
- No salt in the dough. Salt the cooking water instead, as with boxed pasta.
- Dry the pasta completely. Pieces that are still soft in the middle can mold in a sealed container. If you're unsure, break one open; it should be hard and the same color all the way through. Once fully dry, it keeps at least a month.
- Freezing also works. Freeze the fresh pieces on the sheet pan until firm, then bag them. Cook from frozen, adding a minute or two.
- Clean the press after the dough left in it has dried. Leave the plate and auger out for a few hours, then the dough flakes out with the cleaning tool. Wet dough just smears.
- The press's plates are plastic, not bronze, so the surface comes out smoother than artisan dried pasta. Rigatoni's ridges make up for most of that.
- Rigatoni or large macaroni for chunky sauces. The tubes catch chickpeas and crumbled sausage. Fusilli for thick sauces without big pieces.
- The nutrition is calculated for 500 g of semolina split into 6 portions, not counting dusting semolina.
- Made for [harissa baked feta pasta](https://calebstew.art/recipes/mains/harissa-baked-feta-pasta/). For ribbons and filled pasta, use the egg-based [homemade pasta](https://calebstew.art/recipes/basics/homemade-pasta/) instead.
